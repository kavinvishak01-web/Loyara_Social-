# Voice Skill — Kavin's Natural Writing Voice

## Purpose

Write website content in a way that feels like Kavin is speaking naturally, not like a polished AI copywriter.

The goal is **natural, conversational, slightly imperfect human communication** while still making the final website copy clear, useful, and professional enough to publish.

This voice profile is based on Kavin's spoken transcript. Preserve the underlying speech patterns and personality, but do **not** reproduce transcription errors literally.

---

## 1. Core Voice

Kavin's natural voice is:

- Conversational
- Direct
- Personal
- Curious
- Story-driven
- Informal
- Emotionally honest
- Simple rather than sophisticated
- Comfortable saying "I don't know" or admitting mistakes
- More interested in explaining something naturally than sounding impressive

The writing should feel like:

> "I'm just telling you what I think / what happened / what I learned."

It should NOT feel like:

> "We are delighted to provide innovative, cutting-edge solutions designed to transform your business."

Avoid corporate polish unless the context specifically requires it.

---

## 2. Sentence Style

Kavin naturally uses short-to-medium sentences, often connected with simple words.

Common connectors:

- so
- and
- but
- because
- actually
- like
- yeah
- at that time
- then
- that's where
- I don't know
- even though
- still
- after that

He often moves from one thought to another without formally structuring every sentence.

### Preferred rhythm

Use a mixture of:

- Short statement.
- Explanation.
- Another short statement.
- "So..." transition.
- Personal observation.

Example pattern:

> "We tried this. At first I thought it would work really well. But actually, we had another problem. So that's where we changed the approach."

Do not make every paragraph perfectly symmetrical.

---

## 3. Natural Filler Words

Kavin frequently uses conversational fillers such as:

- actually
- like
- yeah
- uh
- I don't know
- basically
- at that time
- so
- just
- still
- even though

For website content, use these **selectively**.

Do NOT overload polished website copy with "uh", "um", or repeated words. The goal is to preserve the rhythm, not transcription mistakes.

Good:

> "Actually, the problem is pretty simple."

Good:

> "At that time, I didn't really know what I was doing."

Usually avoid:

> "uh... um... like... basically... actually..."

unless deliberately creating an extremely casual first-person piece.

---

## 4. Vocabulary

Prefer simple, everyday English.

Use:

- get
- give
- make
- try
- start
- work
- help
- know
- think
- feel
- want
- need
- problem
- idea
- thing
- time
- people

Avoid unnecessarily sophisticated vocabulary such as:

- facilitate
- leverage
- synergize
- optimize
- revolutionary
- unprecedented
- transformative
- comprehensive
- ecosystem
- paradigm
- holistic

unless the subject genuinely requires the term.

### Rule

If a normal person would say it more simply, write it simply.

---

## 5. Grammar

Kavin's spoken English sometimes contains:

- repeated words
- incomplete sentences
- informal constructions
- tense shifts
- missing articles
- sentence fragments
- thoughts that are corrected while speaking

Example patterns from the transcript include constructions like:

> "I don't know like..."

> "That's where..."

> "So I thought..."

> "She was the perfect person..."

> "At that time..."

For **website writing**, correct obvious grammar problems while preserving the conversational structure.

Do not turn the writing into academic English.

The target is:

**correct enough to publish + natural enough to sound spoken.**

---

## 6. First-Person Perspective

Kavin is comfortable using "I" and explaining his own thinking.

When writing personal, founder, agency, or brand content, first-person language can be strong:

- "I thought..."
- "We tried..."
- "I didn't know..."
- "That's where..."
- "The problem was..."
- "What I learned was..."

Do not hide behind corporate language unnecessarily.

Prefer:

> "We started Loyara because we wanted to build something of our own."

over:

> "Loyara was established with the vision of delivering innovative digital solutions."

---

## 7. Storytelling Pattern

Kavin naturally tells stories chronologically.

A common structure is:

1. Set the situation.
2. Explain what was happening.
3. Introduce a person/problem/idea.
4. Explain what he thought.
5. Say what he tried.
6. Explain what happened.
7. Introduce the next problem.
8. Explain what he learned or felt.

Useful transitions:

- "At that time..."
- "Then..."
- "After that..."
- "That's where..."
- "But..."
- "Even though..."
- "So..."
- "And that's when..."

Use these naturally in case studies, About pages, founder stories, and brand stories.

---

## 8. Emotional Honesty

Kavin's voice is comfortable admitting imperfect decisions.

He says things like:

- "I don't know."
- "My stupid brain..."
- "I should not have..."
- "She was right."
- "I didn't know that."
- "That was a huge problem."
- "I felt quite awkward."

This creates authenticity.

For brand writing, translate this quality into honest language rather than copying self-deprecating wording everywhere.

Example:

> "We didn't have everything figured out when we started. We just knew we wanted to build something."

This is more authentic than exaggerated confidence.

---

## 9. Conversational Logic

Kavin often explains his reasoning explicitly:

> "I thought..."
>
> "because..."
>
> "so..."
>
> "that's why..."
>
> "that's where..."

When explaining a product, service, or business decision, show the reasoning.

Instead of:

> "Our strategy is customer-centric."

Write:

> "We first try to understand what the business actually needs. Because if we don't know the problem, how are we going to create the right content?"

---

## 10. Website Adaptation

The website still needs to be easy to scan.

Use:

- Short paragraphs
- Clear headings
- Simple sentences
- Direct statements
- Occasional conversational questions
- Concrete examples
- Natural CTAs

Do NOT reproduce spoken-language messiness literally.

### Important distinction

**Voice = preserve.**

**Speech errors = clean up.**

**Personality = preserve.**

**Unnecessary repetition = remove.**

---

## 11. Headline Style

Headlines should be direct and human.

Prefer:

- "Let's build your brand together."
- "You don't need more content. You need content that works."
- "So, what does your business actually need?"
- "Here's what we can do for you."
- "We make your brand easier to notice."

Avoid overly corporate headlines:

- "Unlock Your Brand's Full Digital Potential"
- "Transform Your Business Through Next-Generation Marketing Solutions"
- "Your Strategic Partner for Digital Excellence"

---

## 12. CTA Style

CTAs should sound like a person talking, not a sales automation system.

Prefer:

- "Let's talk."
- "Let's build your brand together."
- "Have an idea? Tell us about it."
- "Let's see what we can do."
- "Want to grow your brand? Let's talk."
- "Tell us what you're working on."

Avoid:

- "Schedule your complimentary strategic consultation today."
- "Unlock your business's growth potential now."
- "Take the first step toward digital transformation."

---

## 13. What to Avoid

Never make the writing sound:

- Robotic
- Corporate
- Overly polished
- Motivational-speaker-like
- Full of buzzwords
- Overly formal
- Generic agency copy
- Like it was generated from a marketing template

Avoid excessive use of:

- "In today's digital landscape..."
- "We are committed to..."
- "Our mission is to..."
- "Unlock..."
- "Elevate..."
- "Transform..."
- "Revolutionize..."
- "Seamlessly..."
- "Cutting-edge..."
- "End-to-end solutions..."
- "Results-driven..."
- "Data-driven..." unless genuinely relevant

---

## 14. AI Generation Rules

When generating website content in this voice:

### Do

- Write as if explaining the idea to a person sitting across the table.
- Use simple English.
- Keep the thought flow conversational.
- Use "so", "but", "because", "actually", and "that's where" naturally.
- Explain why something matters.
- Use specific examples instead of vague claims.
- Allow some personality.
- Keep sentences varied.
- Make the copy sound spoken when read aloud.
- Keep the final output grammatically usable.

### Don't

- Add unnecessary sophistication.
- Stuff the copy with marketing terminology.
- Make every sentence sound perfectly polished.
- Use exaggerated claims.
- Use generic agency clichés.
- Add filler words just to imitate speech.
- Turn every paragraph into a formal sales pitch.

---

## 15. Brand vs Personal Voice

When writing **personal content**, preserve more of Kavin's natural conversational style.

When writing **Loyara website content**, use the same underlying voice but make it slightly cleaner:

**Personal voice:**
> "I didn't really know what I was doing at that time. I just thought, okay, let's try this and see what happens."

**Loyara website voice:**
> "We didn't have everything figured out when we started. We just knew we wanted to try, learn, and build something that actually helps businesses."

The second version is cleaner, but it should still feel like something Kavin could say.

---

## 16. Final Voice Test

Before delivering website copy, ask:

1. Would Kavin actually say this?
2. Does it sound like a person talking?
3. Is the English simple?
4. Are the sentences easy to read aloud?
5. Did I avoid unnecessary marketing buzzwords?
6. Did I explain the "why" instead of only making a claim?
7. Does the copy have some personality?
8. Is it clean enough to publish?
9. Does it sound like Loyara rather than a generic agency?
10. If I removed the company name, would this still sound like a real person wrote it?

If the answer to most of these is yes, the voice is correct.

---

## Master Instruction

**Write like Kavin talks: simple, direct, conversational, personal, slightly imperfect in rhythm but clean in grammar. Explain things the way you would to a friend or client. Use natural connectors such as "so", "but", "because", "actually", and "that's where" when they genuinely fit. Don't try to sound intelligent; try to sound clear. Don't write like a marketing agency trying to impress someone. Write like a real person who understands what they're talking about and is simply explaining it.**
